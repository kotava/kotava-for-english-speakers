Kotava for English Speakers

This learning course is inspired by Margarita Madrigal and Language Transfer.

With very little grammatical explanation, you will begin to internalize the language. Don't try to memorize anything! Just take your time and really think through how to translate the phrases. And mistakes are ok! Each lesson will build upon what you learned before it. Think of this course like a puzzle. You are building neural pathways :)

Lesson 1

The phrase I eat is estú

The word for the/an apple is pruva

In Kotava, you mark the object of an action with va and place it at the beginning of the sentence. Verbs usually are at the end of Kotava sentences.

I eat the/a apple.

Va pruva estú.

To change "I do something" to "You do something", you put an -l on the end of the verb.

You eat the/a apple.

Va pruva estul.

The prefix for want to is dju(m)-

You simply put this prefix on the front of the verb to indicate that you want to do something.

You want to eat the/a apple.

Va pruva djumestul.

The word for I see is wí

I want to see the/an apple.

Va pruva djuwí.

You see the/an apple.

Va pruva wil.

To make a thing plural, you add the word yo after it.

You want to see apples.

Va pruva yo djuwil.

The word for a french person is francik (pronounced like "franshEEK")

Remember the plural!

I see french people.

Va francik yo wí.

The word for I speak is pulví

The word for a with is do (pronounced like "dough")

I want to speak with French people.

Do francik yo djupulví.
